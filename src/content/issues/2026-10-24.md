Three posts, each deciding where to pay for a boundary. OpenAI stops paying for isolation with dedicated capacity and enforces it with throttling instead. Cloudflare pays for a geographic boundary with key distribution rather than data placement. Meta pays for cold-start coverage by changing what an item is in the model.

---

## OpenAI gives up dedicated containers and polices the hot keys

OpenAI's storage platform, Habitat, serves over 1 billion ChatGPT users from 40 regions, holds more than 500 PB, and sees reads above 90% of requests. The first design let clients choose logical partition keys, which let use cases collide. Cosmos DB limits are enforced per physical partition, so noisy neighbors were routine, and partition splits halved per-partition throughput while total provisioned RU/s stayed the same.

The fix was to move from many dedicated containers back to one shared multi-tenant container by default, with throttling that hits the hottest keys first and QoS tiers that shed offline bulk work before interactive traffic. Dedicated containers had isolated workloads, but every team became a capacity planner and launches could outgrow what was pre-provisioned. A Valkey lookaside cache near compute is invalidated from the Cosmos change feed instead of relying on TTLs, and the post reports global p50 read latency falling from roughly 120 ms to roughly 10 ms. The proxy was rewritten from Python to Rust, and the longer-term plan is an in-house exabyte-scale store on RocksDB Cloud with Raft replication.

The cost of the shared model is that isolation now depends on throttling policy being right. The post also describes a co-managed relationship with the Azure team after outages in late 2024, which is a useful data point on what "managed service" means at this scale. Several figures come without baselines. [source →](https://openai.com/index/scaling-storage-one-billion-users-part-two/)

## Cloudflare keeps ciphertext global and plaintext regional

Cloudflare's post-quantum Geo Key Manager addresses two constraints at once: long-lived encrypted data is exposed to harvest-now-decrypt-later attacks, and customers want decryption keys usable only inside an approved geography even though the network replicates data everywhere. Wrapped private keys are distributed globally through Quicksilver, and only edge servers in the allowed region can unwrap them.

Fixed 256-bit regional root keys derive per-service, per-region, per-epoch HPKE key pairs, with ML-KEM-768 combined with X25519 for key agreement. A server proves its hardware identity with a TPM, an inventory system maps that identity to a location, and a provisioning service issues a token scoped to that region's root keys. Root keys live in the Linux kernel keyring, not on disk, and the key service fails closed when a key is missing or a check fails. Cloudflare chose fixed regional keys over attribute-based encryption because no production-ready post-quantum ABE scheme exists and customers need only a modest number of regions.

The costs are stated plainly. Volatile keys mean reprovisioning after every restart, failing closed trades availability for security, and soft rotation leaves older epochs usable until a hard rotation removes them. The product is in closed beta, with general availability planned for Q1 2027. [source →](https://blog.cloudflare.com/post-quantum-geo-key-manager/)

## Meta swaps item IDs for a quantized token vocabulary

Ads ranking has relied on per-item IDs and click history, which leaves new ads and tail inventory with little training signal. Meta encodes each ad, product or page with a pre-trained multimodal model, then a three-stage tokenizer decomposes the embedding into subspaces and quantizes each against a codebook, producing a short sequence of discrete tokens. Related items share tokens, so a new item inherits learned signal from its neighbors.

The more transferable detail is how they iterate. Across eight tokenizer versions, intrinsic measures such as cluster purity, distribution balance and codebook utilization correlated strongly with CTR prediction accuracy, so the tokenizer can be tuned without a full training cycle. Paired with Meta's GEM and Adaptive Ranking Model, the post reports a +0.35% conversion lift across surfaces, from internal data measured in February 2026.

Limits: the lift is one internal number with no public baseline, and a stable vocabulary shifts the burden to keeping tokenizer quality high. Fully verbalized tokenization gives the richest representation but its context windows are impractical at ads scale, so Meta is exploring compact verbalized tokens combined with semantic IDs. [source →](https://engineering.fb.com/2026/10/19/ml-applications/genrecs-tokenization-transforming-ads-recommendation-at-meta-with-stable-model-vocabulary/)
